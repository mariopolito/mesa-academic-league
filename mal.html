Points, streak, covered

Points

Every right answer scores. A harder question is worth more, the first time you ever get a question right is worth +5 on top, and setting a card aside as mastered is worth +15. Your streak adds 2 × the streak to each answer, so a long clean run is worth far more than the same questions answered one at a time.

Streak

How many questions you have answered correctly in a row without repeating yourself. A question you have already had right during this streak still scores 2, but it will not grow the streak — covering more of the guide is the point, not answering the same question twenty times. Miss one and the streak goes back to zero, and every question is fair game again.

Covered

How much of the study guide you have answered correctly at least once — questions, capitals, nicknames, mottos, spelling words, verb tenses, the gods and the tip-offs. Every event that grades an answer counts: the typing drill, matching, the quiz, the buzzer, scrimmage and the category board. Bank questions count while their quarter is selected; the memorize sets are filed under no quarter, so once you have them they stay counted.

Mastered cards

Separate from Covered, and it is your call: the I know this one button on the back of a flashcard sets that card aside, worth +15, and you can hide the ones you have set aside so the deck shrinks as you learn it. Getting that question wrong anywhere in the app hands the card straight back — and takes the 15 with it.

Eight ways to train

Start with the reference sheets and flashcards to load the material, then work up through the timed drills to a full scrimmage

The reference sheets are what you memorize on the bus. The questions with no single short answer are spread across the events they belong to: the matching, grouping and ordering ones are in Matching, the multi-part fill-ins are in the Typing drill, and the handful that are genuinely open-ended — “explain the difference between…” — are in the Buzzer drill.

Scrimmage

Random question, random format, random clock

A real match doesn't warn you what kind of question is next — this doesn't either. Eight formats take turns: multiple choice, typed answer, buzz-and-reveal, true/false, spelling, pairs, capitals, and the interactive sets. Never the same one twice in a row, and each one runs its own clock.

No quarters selected.

A scrimmage draws its questions from the quarters you have selected, and right now that is none of them. Turn on a quarter up in the scoreboard — All switches every one of them back on.

Buzzer drill

A question, a running clock, and an honest self-grade

Say your answer out loud, then buzz to reveal. Be honest about whether you had it — that's how the streak means something. Space buzzes, 1 is got it, 2 is missed. The tip-offs run on a six-second clock, the way the guide means them to; everything else gets twelve.

Flashcards

Click the card to flip it — or take a hint first

Most cards have a hint that points at the approach without giving the answer. Shuffle each time so you learn the answer, not the order. Everything mixes the question bank in with the capitals, mottos, spelling and tenses — closest to what a real match throws at you. A question that asks for four things at once is dealt out as four separate cards, and a matching set becomes one card per pair — a card should never ask you to match a whole column. That is what the Matching board is for.

Quiz

Four choices, no clock, as many questions as you want

Each wrong answer is the thing that question is actually confused with — mitosis next to meiosis, complement next to compliment — so knowing it roughly won't get you there.

Matching

Pair them up, group them, or drag them into order

The study guide’s own matching and grouping questions, played rather than read — match the columns, sort each item into its category, drag a list into order, tick every one that belongs, and find out exactly what you missed. The memorize boards underneath are the pure-recall sets: six pairs at a time, and a pair you get wrong goes back on the board.

Typing drill

Type it from memory

The three memorize-and-recall drills in one place: the fifty capitals both directions, the spelling words read aloud with the word blanked out of its sentence, and the fourteen gods by name or by what they rule. Spelling counts for the letters, not the accents — “st paul” and “St. Paul” both pass. Nothing advances until you say so.

Reference sheets

The parts of the study guide that are pure memorization

Capitals, state nicknames and mottos, the verb tenses, the category legend, the mythology table and the tip-off list. Everything here is fair game for a tip-off, and clicking a row gives you the part that makes it stick.

Category board

The clue is a statement. Your answer is a question

Every clue on this board is written as a statement, and all four options come back as questions — “What is Montgomery?”, “Who is Zeus?” — the way a response works on the buzzer show. Four columns are categories from the question bank, labeled with the two-letter code from the legend on the Reference sheet. Three tiles per column, worth 200, 400 and 600 for level 1, 2 and 3 — pick one, answer before the clock runs out, and it is marked off the board. The other two columns come from the reference sheets — capitals, mythology, state nicknames and mottos, verb tenses, vocabulary, tip-offs — each labeled with which way it runs, and they stay open no matter which quarters are selected.
